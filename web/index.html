About this program

Minaret computes the Islamic prayer hours, the direction of the Qibla and converts between the Hijri and Gregorian calendars. This is a web version of the Windows program Minaret by Kamal Abdali (1987–1998); the calculations follow the original Visual Basic source (kept in this repository under minaret-master/vb6-src).

Times shown

  • Fajr – dawn: sun is below the horizon by the chosen angle (or fixed minutes before sunrise). In Options you may enter several angles (say 15 and 18); the tables then show Fajr15 and Fajr18 columns. The same works for Isha.
  • Shuruq – sunrise (sun's centre 0.83° below the horizon).
  • Ishraq – begins when the sun has risen 1.4° above the horizon after sunrise (adjustable in Options).
  • Zuhr – solar noon.
  • Asr 1× and Asr 2× – afternoon: the shadow equals once (Shafi‘i, Maliki, Hanbali) or twice (Hanafi) the object's length, plus the noon shadow. Both are always shown.
  • Asr Makruh – begins when the sun is still 2.3° above the horizon before sunset, and lasts till Maghrib (adjustable).
  • Maghrib – sunset (a few methods use the sun's angle below the horizon instead). Isha – night: sun below the horizon by the chosen angle, or fixed minutes after Maghrib (some methods use more minutes in Ramadan).
  • Calculation method – Options lists the conventions used in various countries and institutions; choose Custom to set every angle and interval yourself.

A * means that the event does not occur on that day at that latitude. Year 0 gives a perpetual schedule (average of a four-year cycle, valid for any year). Dates 1901–2200 A.D. are supported.

Hijri dates

The Hijri month is assumed to begin when the crescent is 1.08 days after the astronomical new moon, as in the original program. Actual local moon-sighting announcements can differ by a day.

Privacy

Everything is computed on your device; settings are kept in your browser only.
